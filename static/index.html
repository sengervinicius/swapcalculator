📊 Hedge Cost Methodology
Core Formula (USD → BRL)
The hedge cost/benefit is calculated as the difference between the implied USD rate in Brazil and the actual USD rate:
Hedge Benefit (bps) = (Cupom Cambial − SOFR) × 100
Cupom Cambial = Implied USD rate in Brazil (from B3 futures)
SOFR = Secured Overnight Financing Rate (actual USD rate)
Data Sources
Cupom Cambial
B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão) FREE
SOFR
FRED (St. Louis Fed) FREE
Example Calculation
Current Market (Illustrative)
Cupom Cambial (5Y): 5.25% p.a.
SOFR: 4.30% p.a.
────────────────────────
Hedge Benefit: (5.25 − 4.30) × 100 = +95 bps
A positive value means you benefit from hedging (the implied USD rate in Brazil exceeds actual USD funding). A negative value would mean a hedging cost.
Why Cupom Cambial?
The Cupom Cambial already incorporates the convertibility premium — the cost of USD being "trapped" in Brazil's financial system vs. freely tradeable offshore USD. This is why:
Theoretical max gain = CDI − SOFR ≈ 900 bps
Actual hedge gain = Cupom Cambial − SOFR ≈ 50-150 bps
Difference = Convertibility Premium (already priced in)
Additional Components (Not Included)
⚠️ This calculation represents the expected hedge cost/benefit based on current market rates. It does not include: (1) Transaction costs (bid-offer spreads), (2) Slippage from discrete hedging, (3) Model uncertainty. For a complete analysis, consider using the manual override with your institution's specific hedging parameters.
Based on CIP (Covered Interest Parity) principles. See: Patry & Mozley (2021) "Future Hedging Costs: Back to Basics"
